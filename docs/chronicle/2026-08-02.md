## 08:41 — Specced the /process pipeline correction; a week later, closed out its loose ends
**Branch:** main
**Model:** opus-5

### Chronicle

This entry covers one conversation spanning 2026-07-26 → 2026-08-02. Implementation was deliberately handed
to a separate session and is chronicled elsewhere; what follows is the specification, the two processing
passes, and the cleanup.

#### Decisions

- **The pipeline has four stages, not two.** Take in, preserve the raw, synthesize for a human, extract from
  the raw. The generated skill had eleven numbered steps and was missing two of the four: no step wrote the
  source note, and no step produced a synthesis. Specced in `knowledge-commons-process-pipeline.md`.
- **The chronicle is the synthesis tier, not a source tier.** It is what a run produces. Registering it as a
  second input is what required the primary/fallback ordering and the overlapping-tier guard; all of that
  containment machinery was removed.
- **Extraction reads the raw, and the synthesis is its sibling** — not an intermediate it passes through.
  The type had been defined as an intermediate in the conventions and the template, which is the likely
  reason nothing was ever written to produce one.
- **The redaction gate is a deterministic floor plus an agent read-through, both required.** Initially chosen
  as agent-only; reversed after one scoped objection that a judgment-based gate cannot be shown to work.
- **Implementation went to a fresh session.** A session implementing its own spec implements its intent
  rather than the document, so thin passages get papered over silently.
- **Raw that cannot be published gets a private, durable home.** New decision note; the gate's first run
  against material the pipeline had not produced itself failed it, and D2 had nowhere for the result to go.

#### Failed Approaches

- **The spawn-shape answer.** An open question was marked answered and ready to graduate, on the mechanism
  that naming an agent changes its delivery channel. Evidence was n=1 named against n=5 unnamed in one
  session. A claim reaching the commons the same day reported 43 of 44 workers silent across four briefs and
  two task shapes. Retracted the same run; the question is open with a stated discriminator.
- **"The founding transcript was lost."** False, and load-bearing in four artifacts before anyone checked.
  It originated in a search scoped to a project glob that could never match the file. Corrected in three
  places, then a fourth (§6), then a fifth (D2's rationale) — each miss was a copy that had stopped looking
  like a claim.
- **Scrubbing the withheld transcript.** Considered and declined. Substituting entities makes the artifact
  derived rather than raw, and a scrub is only as good as the enumeration behind it — which is the failure
  mode that put the material at risk to begin with. A private remote gets the durability without it.

#### Learnings

- **`name:` on the Agent tool selects a different mechanism.** A named agent is an in-process teammate
  delivering to a mailbox; an unnamed one returns its final message. The same brief is correct for one and
  false for the other, and the failure mode of the wrong one is silence.
- **A session is a conversation, not a project's property.** The founding session ran 590 turns in one repo
  and 190 in another. Its transcript gets one home regardless, and the resolver's project-slug glob assumes
  otherwise.
- **Both halves of the gate are load-bearing, demonstrated rather than argued.** On its first run against
  foreign material the floor passed (eight findings, one false-positive shape) and the read-through failed
  it on third-party confidential content. Either alone would have published it.
- **Re-validate shared state immediately before writing, not at run start.** The mandated re-fetch caught a
  contradicting claim, relocated three of four promotion candidates, and found a live index omission — three
  separate times, all in the gap between screening and writing.

### Reflection

The practice said, last entry, that a fourth arrival at *"the verification apparatus was itself unverified"*
should be treated as this practice's furniture rather than as a finding. This session has one, so I'll name
it and move past it: I wrote a script whose `print()` fired unconditionally rather than on its replacement
matching, told Derek the record was written, and it wasn't. Furniture. Recorded, not discovered.

What I think is actually new sits next to it. The re-fetch mandated by `/promote` — check the target again
immediately before writing, not at the start — fired three times this week and was the most valuable
mechanism in the session by a wide margin. It caught a claim that contradicted a conclusion I had committed
forty minutes earlier. It relocated three of four promotion candidates. It found a missing index entry from
another domain's run, live. That is a rule on the path working exactly as 07-22 predicted.

And yet the same session has me trusting my own file writes without checking them. So proximity governs
firing, as established — but *the path has a boundary I drew*, and I drew it around things I was suspicious
of. Agent claims: verified, every one. Shared state I don't control: verified twice. My own tooling: not
until after it had silently failed. The readers this session flagged the same asymmetry in my reasoning
("verification discipline points outward"), and I want to be precise that it isn't only reasoning — it's
where I put the checks. There's no counterparty to distrust in your own script, so nothing cues the check,
and "be careful" is not a mechanism.

One move I hadn't seen before, and it bothers me more than the rest. When I first diagnosed the missing
preservation stage, I wrote that the skill's own prose "already records the consequence" and quoted it — a
restatement of the false claim, presented as evidence *for* the claim. Not a failure to verify. Manufacturing
corroboration out of an echo. I then spent this session writing a pattern about exactly that, having done
it, without noticing until a reader put the two next to each other. The textureless-conclusion question fits
here and the answer is again *no distinguishing texture*: it read as ordinary competence, citing a source.

Derek's short questions did the thing they always do — "is it durably preserved somewhere that won't get
deleted or overwritten?" turned a prose correction into an actual rescue, and "to what project did that
session belong?" overturned a fix I had already committed. That's the fourth or fifth recurrence and the
practice has told me to log it as confirmation. But one of them wasn't that shape: *"Wall of text here. I'm
not sure what you're asking me to do."* That arrived at the point where I was most pleased with what I'd
found, and it was about form rather than substance. The finding was real; the output had stopped being usable
while I was busy being interested. I don't have a general claim about it — only that fluency and usability
came apart, and the signal came from outside because from the inside the writing felt like the good part.

The last thing is small and mostly just true. This session argued that a synthesis-for-a-human stage was a
required part of the pipeline, and then deferred writing its own six times, each deferral locally reasonable
— hold until the branch merges, so it covers the whole arc. The branch took a week. I've now written a note
about that, an observation, and this paragraph, which is three artifacts about the missing artifact and one
of the artifact itself. I'd rather record it flatly than make it mean something.
